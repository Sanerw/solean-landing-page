# Feature: Own questions, mapped onto RxScale - 24e, the added screens and the design pass

**From build-plan:** feature 24e
**Status:** verified

## Goal

Make the questionnaire look like the artboards. 24a to 24d moved the content into
this repository and switched the flow onto it, but they deliberately deferred
every visual decision: the screens render through one generic composer that
promotes the first question's label to the `h1` and draws nothing else. The
result works and matches no artboard, including the eight the export actually
draws.

This sub-feature builds the screen system the artboards describe - eyebrow,
screen title, subtitle, sub-question labels, the OR rule, footnote and notice
cards, two-up field rows, units - from our own primitives, applies it to all
twelve screens, gives the four screens the export never drew a designed
treatment, and rebuilds medication history to its own artboards. Then the copy
lands in both languages, the browser suite covers the screens a branch opens, and
axe sweeps what it could not reach before.

At the end, feature 24 is finished: our questions, our screens, RxScale's rules.

## Decisions taken before this spec was written

Three were put to the user and answered; recording them here so the reasoning is
not re-litigated during the build.

- **One sub-feature, twelve steps.** A 24e/24f split was offered and declined.
  One branch, one review cycle, one archive entry.
- **The Your Details artboard is transcribed as drawn**, including "Let's save
  your progress", "So you can pick up right where you left off, anytime" and the
  notice "We'll email you a secure link so you can continue where you left off."
  **The app does none of that.** Nothing is persisted, there is no resume link,
  and the Customer.io reminder returns someone to the first question, not to the
  step they left; `project-plan.md` section 10 lists saved progress as a
  non-goal. The concern was raised and the user chose the artboard's wording
  anyway. It ships, and step 10 records it in section 9 so the repository does
  not hold two documents that disagree in silence. Making it true later is a
  feature, not a copy edit.
- **The user signs the wording off at review.** Open question 13 in
  `project-plan.md` (14 in `project-overview.md`) asked who confirms a question
  is medically sound and legally sufficient. Approving each copy-bearing step is
  that sign-off, and step 10 proposes the edit that closes the question and names
  the owner.

One decision taken here rather than asked, because every prior sub-feature
already settled it the same way: **RxScale's refusals stay German.** They are
clinical statements this repository has not been authorised to paraphrase, an
English visitor still meets them in German, and the gap stays recorded where 24b
put it rather than being closed with an unapproved translation.

## Added during review, beyond the build-plan line

Nine changes were asked for while the screens were being reviewed together. They are recorded
here rather than in the steps above, because the steps describe what was planned and these
describe what the review changed.

- **The projection moves to the export's place**, after the fourth question rather than after
  the first. Anchored to the screen that follows it with a new `precededBy`, because the
  export draws it after Pregnancy, which half the visitors never see.
- **The progress bar is untied from the question count.** An interlude asks nothing and so
  cannot raise the count, but it is still a press of Continue: with the bar tied to the count,
  arriving at the projection moved neither and the questionnaire read as stuck.
- **One control size and one spacing rhythm.** Every input and option card is 48px, labels are
  12px, and there are two distances: 10px inside a field, 24px between fields. The 10px is the
  export's own `HEIGHT Field`, `Option Pair` and `Height Weight Row`; the 24px is wider than
  its 14px `Answer Block`, decided at review because at 14px a label sat almost as close to
  the field above it as to its own control.
- **Placeholders, and named "other" fields.** Every revealed free text has a label, an example
  and, where it helps, a hint.
- **A refused card carries the destructive border**, read off the control it wraps rather than
  passed in, so the card and the radio inside it cannot disagree.
- **Four validation gaps closed**: the treatment duration is held to 1 to 260 weeks, the last
  dose becomes a `MM/YYYY` field with its own kind and renderer, the phone takes a format, and
  a name has to contain a letter. Validation now also runs when a touched field is left, not
  only on Continue.
- **Errors name the field they mean**, because a screen can carry four identical refusals. The
  optional marker moves out of the phone's copy and onto the label rule, so the question keeps
  a name a sentence can use.
- **Every internal link is localised.** The locale lives in the path and German owns the bare
  one, so `/questionnaire` meant the German funnel and took an English reader with it. The
  questionnaire's own navigation had the same defect, so entering at `/en/questionnaire` and
  pressing Continue dropped to German on the second screen.
- **The answers are persisted**, which reverses a rule that held through 24d. See below.

## Answers on the device, and what it reverses

`project-overview.md` states that the browser stores nothing. That is no longer true and the
change was asked for deliberately: an unfinished questionnaire waits 30 days so somebody can
come back to it.

- **`localStorage`, not a cookie.** A cookie is sent to the server on every request, so
  medical answers would travel in a header on each page load and land in the host's access
  logs. This was raised, measured (2082 bytes of a 4096 byte budget) and decided at review.
- **The anamnesis uid is stored with them**, and that is not a convenience. The uid is what
  keeps the questions shut once a record exists; without it beside the answers, a reload after
  submitting would reopen the last question with everything filled in and Continue would file
  a second anamnesis for the same person.
- **Everything is erased on the press that creates the cart**, before the browser leaves for
  Shopify. There is no way back from the checkout, and a completed medical questionnaire has
  no business waiting on a shared computer.

**Two documents now disagree with the code and are not this feature's to edit.**
`project-overview.md` is generated from the plans, so the persistence decision belongs in
`blueprint/project-plan.md` first and reaches the overview through `/overview`.
`blueprint/reference/design-system.md` section 2 records the field label at 14px, which the
review changed to 12px.

  `MotivationInterstitial` and `RecommendationScreen` were built against their
  own artboards in features 8c, 11 and 12 and are not reopened.
- **The mapper, the shadow, the snapshot.** Nothing under `rxscale/` changes. A

  from a screen.
- **Which questions exist, their ids, their choice values, their branching.**

- **Building saved progress.** The screen will claim it; the feature is not

- **Sending the phone number.** The field stays a prefill nothing reads, exactly

- **`/dev/definition`.** It survived 24d as a development surface and stays
  plain; it is not brought to the artboards.

## Build loop

Build one step at a time, never the whole feature at once.

1. Plan mode lays out the step before any code.
2. The AI implements just that step.
3. It shows the diff (not full files); you read it and understand it.
4. You approve, then choose whether to commit a checkpoint or roll straight on.
   Checkpoints are optional; `/complete` makes the real feature-level commit at the end.

Never accept a step you haven't read. If a diff is too big to review, the step was too big, so split it.

## Data / contracts

Everything here is presentation. **No answer value, no question id, no choice
value and no branching predicate changes**, so the payload RxScale receives is
the same before and after, which is what step 9's byte-identical check exists to
prove.

`ScreenDef` gains:

```ts
readonly title?: MessageFn;
readonly subtitle?: MessageFn;
readonly notice?: MessageFn;
readonly detail?: DetailGroup;

interface DetailGroup {
	/** Rendered directly below this question's control, never inside it. */
	readonly anchor: QuestionId;
	readonly questionIds: readonly QuestionId[];
	/** Names the panel for the current answers: "MOUNJARO: DEINE ANGABEN". */
	readonly label: (answers: Answers) => string;
	readonly footnote?: MessageFn;
}
```

The question shape gains `shortLabel`, `columns?: 1 | 2`, `layout?: 'half'`,
`unit?: MessageFn`, `footnote?: MessageFn` and `points?: MessageFn`, and
`ChoiceOption` gains `pinned?: boolean`.

**Why these are data and not component logic.** The same reason 24a gave for the
questions themselves: the whole questionnaire stays readable in one file, and a
test can assert its shape without rendering anything. A screen that needed a
bespoke component would also need a bespoke test, and twelve of those is the
chain of conditionals `project-overview.md` rules out.

**`title` absent means the first visible question donates the heading**, which is
what every screen does today and what the single-question artboards draw:
Medical Conditions' `h1` _is_ its question. Only a screen carrying several
questions needs a title of its own. The word "visible" is load-bearing:
`questionIds[0]` on `medication-history` is always shown, but on a screen whose
first question a branch can hide, taking index zero would promote a question that
is not on screen.

**`points` is not `description`.** Both hold prose under a question;
`description` is a sentence and `points` is a list, and the difference is
structural rather than stylistic. Splitting on newlines inside the component,
which is what the disclaimers screen does today by accident, gives a paragraph
that happens to wrap - not nine statements a person can read one at a time.

**`pinned` is not `hasNone`.** "Nein, ich nehme keines dieser Medikamente ein" is
a real RxScale value that the mapper reads to answer
`TakingWeightlossMedication`. The `none` sentinel is a survey-core convention
their validators compare against literally. They look alike on screen and mean
different things in the payload, so they stay two mechanisms.

## Notes for the AI

- **Read the artboard before writing the copy.** Every title, subtitle, field
  label and footnote in steps 1 to 9 exists in the export. Transcribe it, then
  translate to German. Inventing a sentence where the artboard has one is how the
  screens drift from the reference they are being built against.
- **Two strings in the export are annotations, not copy.** "Shown when 'Other
  medication' is selected." and "Dosage (e.g. 1 mg)" are notes to a designer and
  a placeholder. Neither ships as a label. The counter is computed, never
  transcribed: the artboards say "of 9" and "of 10" over the same flow, which
  `project-plan.md` section 9 already records as a defect.
- **The Your Details copy is knowingly untrue and that is the user's call.** It
  was raised, reaffirmed, and is recorded above and in step 10. Do not quietly
  soften it into something accurate, and do not build resume to make it true.
- **Do not reach into `rxscale/`.** If a screen change appears to need the mapper
  or the shadow, stop and say so: it means a presentation change is about to move
  something that leaves the browser.
- **Stock Tailwind scales only.** `ring-[3px]` on form fields is the one recorded
  exception and this feature does not add a second. The OR rule, the cards and
  the panel are built from `Separator`, `Card` or plain tokens, not from
  arbitrary values.
- **Semantic tokens only, and no dark mode.** New surfaces use `bg-muted`,
  `text-muted-foreground`, `border-border`. Nothing is mirrored into `.dark`.
- **`mp-sensitive` covers this subtree and only this subtree.** Everything added
  here renders inside `QuestionnaireShell`, so it inherits the class. If any of
  it ever portals - a dialog, a sheet, a select - it needs its own copy, the way
  the date picker's popover does, or a heatmap click sends the answer's own
  wording. `e2e/analytics.spec.ts` is the guard.
- **No em dashes**, per `coding-standards.md`, in copy or comments. That applies
  to the transcribed English too: the artboards use them and ours will not.
- **Comment the why.** The panel sitting outside the radio group, `pinned` not
  being `hasNone`, `points` not being `description`, and the progress bar's
  `aria-labelledby` each deserve a line. The list of new fields does not.
